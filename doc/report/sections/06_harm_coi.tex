\section{\texttt{harm-coi}: cones and predicates from the RTL}\label{sec:harmcoi}
\texttt{harm-coi} (\file{tools/harm-coi/}) is a Python tool that reads SystemVerilog RTL and writes the \texttt{coi.json} of \cref{sec:coi}. It is the only part of v4 that reads the design; HARM itself stays source-agnostic.

\subsection{The front end (H5a, D-006)}
Two front ends were compared on the six hand-written fixtures of \ms{H4} in a spike: the elaborated syntax tree of pyslang~\cite{slang}, with our own dataflow and control-dependency walk, and the JSON netlist of yosys~\cite{yosys} with its slang front end. pyslang reproduced all six fixtures exactly. yosys needed a version newer than the one packaged on macOS, lost struct fields and parameters, and lowered the conditions that predicate harvesting needs. The decision was \textbf{pyslang 12.0.0}, pinned (Python $\ge$ 3.11). yosys stays an optional, signal-level cross-check in the tests.

\subsection{Computing cones (H5, D-019)}
The front end elaborates the design and extracts, for every assignment, the direct dependencies of its target, with their register delays:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{data dependencies} from the right-hand side, and \textbf{control dependencies} from the conditions of \code|if|, \code|case| (selector and items) and \code|?:|;
\item a register assigned in a clocked process has delay 1; combinational logic, continuous assignments and port connections have delay 0;
\item a register not assigned on every path holds its value, so it is a source of itself at delay 1;
\item \textbf{asynchronous controls} (\code|always_ff @(posedge clk or posedge rst)| where the body reads \code|rst|) and registers on the falling edge of the sampling clock have depths 0 and 1;
\item \textbf{blocking temporaries}: a variable local to a process is replaced by its sources; a visible signal assigned once in a process and read later in it is also a source at delay 0. This last rule came from a test that was wrong (H5, F2): the yosys cross-check and the simulation oracle both showed that forcing such a temporary changes the output, so filter mode must not prune \code|G(t -> y)|.
\end{itemize}
\texttt{cone.py} maps the design's signals to the names HARM sees: relative to the VCD scope, with \code|::| between sub-scopes. With \code|--vcd|, the trace decides which signals are visible: a packed struct dumped as one vector is one signal, the union of its fields. The closure up to \code|--max-depth| sums the delays along paths and marks saturation.

\paragraph{Too large, never too small.} A cone that is too small would make filter mode prune real behaviour; one that is too large only prunes less. So where the RTL leaves a doubt, the signal goes to \code|unknown|, which HARM treats as in every cone: latches (including an \code|always_comb| output not assigned on every path, since \code|unique| and \code|priority| are not trusted to make a \code|case| complete), registers on another clock or on both edges, \code|inout| ports, tasks, a driver that slang reports but the walk does not model, and every target whose cone reaches an unknown signal within \code|--max-depth|.

\subsection{RTL predicates (H10, D-023)}\label{sec:predicates}
\code|--predicates| also harvests the RTL's own conditions as HARM propositions, written in HARM's syntax and names, with \code|origin="rtl"|:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}P{0.17\textwidth}P{0.42\textwidth}P{0.33\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
Kind & from & predicate \\
\midrule
condition & \code|if (c)|, \code|c ? x : y| & \code|c|, and each atom of a compound \code|c| \\
case label & \code|case (s) L:| & \code|s == L| \\
enum (FSM) value & a variable of an enum type & \code|v == C| for every value \\
comparison & with a constant side, anywhere & the comparison \\
reset value & the first \code|if| of a clocked process whose branch assigns only constants & \code|reg == value| \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Constants are written as sized decimal literals of the signal's width (\code|state == 2'd1|); a 1-bit comparison as the signal or its negation. Predicates on invisible signals, on local variables, or on signals wider than HARM's 511-bit limit (finding F-L5, on the AssertLLM2 design \texttt{sha3}) are dropped and counted. \code|--emit-config| writes a ready-to-run HARM configuration: one context with the predicates, \code|<coi ... mode="rank"/>| pointing at the written file, and \opt{generate-config}'s template and sorts. On the \texttt{counter} fixture (a mod-10 counter with enable):

% example
\begin{lstlisting}
$ harm-coi --top counter --files tests/input/coi/counter/rtl/counter.sv \
    --vcd-scope tb::dut --vcd-recursion 0 --vcd tests/input/coi/counter/trace.vcd \
    --predicates --emit-config $OUT/counter.xml -o $OUT/counter_coi.json
$ grep -E 'prop|coi' $OUT/counter.xml
<prop exp="cnt == 4'd0" loc="a, c, dt" origin="rtl"/>
<prop exp="cnt == 4'd9" loc="a, c, dt" origin="rtl"/>
<prop exp="en" loc="a, c, dt" origin="rtl"/>
<prop exp="rst" loc="a, c, dt" origin="rtl"/>
<coi file="counter_coi.json" mode="rank"/>
$ harm --vcd tests/input/coi/counter/trace.vcd --clk clk --vcd-ss tb::dut --vcd-r 0 \
    --conf $OUT/counter.xml --max-ass 3 --sva --dont-print-ass --dump-to $OUT/counter.txt > /dev/null
$ cat $OUT/counter.txt
always (cnt == 4'b1001 ##1 !(cnt == 4'b1001) |-> cnt == 4'b0)
always (cnt == 4'b1001 && en ##1 1'b1 |-> cnt == 4'b0)
always (rst ##1 1'b1 |-> cnt == 4'b0)
\end{lstlisting}
For GoldMine-style mining, the mode is changed to \code|filter|, with \code|depth| as needed.

\subsection{Hints from the RTL (H22, D-036)}\label{sec:hints}
Predicates give HARM an alphabet, but every target still gets the same template, and every proposition may sit on either side of an implication. In trivergence an LLM supplies the rest from the specification: signal roles, where a proposition may go, templates. \code|--hints FILE.json| writes the same kinds of hint from the RTL, in a file an LLM could also write (\file{doc/schemas/hints.v1.json}; each entry says \code|origin: rtl| or \code|llm|), so that the two sources can be compared and merged. The ground rule is that hints shape roles and timing only, never which signal may explain which: a bug that cuts or adds a connection cannot hide an assertion, as the cone filter can.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Roles.} Each visible signal is an \code|input| (a top input port), a \code|reset| (named by \code|--reset|, or an asynchronous control), a \code|const|, a \code|clock|, \code|unknown| (as in the cone file), \code|comb| (a source at depth~0) or \code|state| (every source at depth $\ge 1$), with a flag \code|control| when an RTL condition reads it.
\item \textbf{Placement.} A predicate may be a consequent only if it reads a \code|comb|, \code|state| or \code|unknown| signal. An assertion whose consequent reads only inputs, resets or constants fails any formal check unless it is a tautology, so such predicates are antecedents only (\code|loc="a, dt"|).
\item \textbf{Templates.} One per class of target: \code+G({..#1&..}|-> P0(1))+ for combinational and unknown targets, \code+G({..#1&..}|=> P0(2))+ for registers, which show their inputs one cycle later.
\end{itemize}
\code|--emit-config| then writes the configuration from the hints file, all of it (\code|--hints-use all|, the default) or the placement only (\code|--hints-use placement|). Without \code|--hints| the output is byte-identical to that of \ms{H10}. An independent oracle recomputes the hints from \code|coi.json| alone; it agrees on the 7 fixtures and on all 21 designs of the miner portfolio's corpus. On the portfolio's measurement, the templates raised the proved assertions on every design that mines in the \code|filter| mode (\texttt{fsm} 8 to 25, \texttt{arbiter} 10 to 18).

\subsection{Relations from the RTL (H24, D-038)}\label{sec:relations}
On the miner portfolio's D1 corpus (12 small designs with 49 planted bugs; a bug counts as caught when a formal tool refutes, on the buggy design, an assertion mined on the correct one), HARM caught 14 of the 37 bugs used for tuning. Traced bug by bug, the misses came from the alphabet, not the mining: signals that are only assigned had no proposition, data relations had none, 1-bit flags had one polarity, and reset assertions (\code+rst |=> ...+) are vacuous under the \code|disable iff (rst)| that trivergence's checks use. With \code|--hints| and \code|--emit-config|, \texttt{harm-coi} therefore also writes \emph{relations} (\code|origin="rtl-relation"|), each with its own domain and template:
\begin{itemize}
\item an unconditional assignment as an equation, with SystemVerilog's widths kept (\code|wrap == (en && (cnt == 4'd9))|), under \code+G({!rst}|-> P0(5))+ for combinational targets and \code+G({!rst}|=> P0(4))+ for registers, whose right-hand side is read one cycle earlier with \code|$past|;
\item a register's value at the end of reset, under \code+G({$fell(rst)}|-> P0(3))+;
\item a conditional assignment's equation and, for a register assigned under a condition, \code|$stable(x)|, in single-cycle decision trees (\code|1D,3W,3A|) that find the condition;
\item both polarities of a 1-bit flag.
\end{itemize}
On the \texttt{counter} fixture:
% example
\begin{lstlisting}
$ harm-coi --top counter --files tests/input/coi/counter/rtl/counter.sv \
    --vcd-scope tb::dut --vcd-recursion 0 --vcd tests/input/coi/counter/trace.vcd \
    --predicates --reset rst --hints $OUT/hints.json \
    --emit-config $OUT/counter.xml -o $OUT/coi.json
$ grep -E 'rtl-relation|fell' $OUT/counter.xml
<prop exp="wrap == (en &amp;&amp; (cnt == 4'd9))" loc="c, 5" origin="rtl-relation"/>
<prop exp="$stable(cnt)" loc="c, 7" origin="rtl-relation"/>
<template dtLimits="5D,3W,15A,-0.1E,U" exp="G({$fell(rst)}|-&gt; P0(3))"/>
$ harm --vcd tests/input/coi/counter/trace.vcd --clk clk --vcd-ss tb::dut --vcd-r 0 \
    --conf $OUT/counter.xml --sva --dont-print-ass --dump-to $OUT/counter.txt > /dev/null
$ grep -E 'past|stable|fell' $OUT/counter.txt | head -3
always (!rst && en |=> cnt == ($past((cnt == 4'b1001 ? 4'b0 : cnt + 4'b1)) & 4'b1111))
always (!en && !rst |=> $stable(cnt))
always (cnt == 4'b0 && en |=> cnt == ($past((cnt == 4'b1001 ? 4'b0 : cnt + 4'b1)) & 4'b1111))
\end{lstlisting}
Relations restate the RTL. They catch a design that has changed since it was mined, which is what trivergence and the portfolio's bug hunts ask; they cannot catch a bug that the mined RTL already has. With relations, HARM caught 34 of the 37 tuning bugs and 11 of the 12 held out, 45 of 49 (\ms{H24}); 46 after \ms{H25} and 47 after \ms{H26}, losing none it caught before; on the portfolio's three larger designs the mining loop took 1.1 to 2.5 times longer. Relations became the default with \code|--hints| at \ms{H24}'s sign-off; \code|--no-relations| restores the earlier output. Two oracles check them: every unconditional relation must hold at every cycle of the golden trace after reset, by an evaluator written for the test and, since \ms{H25}, by HARM's own evaluation. Numeric hints (value clusters) were tried and dropped: on D1 they caught nothing the relations missed.

\subsection{Signed RTL (H25, D-039)}
A trace carries no signedness: HARM reads a VCD vector as an unsigned \code|logic|. \texttt{harm-coi} therefore wrote the signed comparison \code|sum > 13'sd2047| as \code|sum > 13'd2047|, true for every negative \code|sum|, and dropped comparisons with negative constants and relations with a sign extension. HARM now reads SystemVerilog's \code|$signed(e)| and \code|$unsigned(e)| (IEEE~1800-2017, 20.5): it evaluates them with D-035's rules, encodes them for Z3 and prints them as written. \texttt{harm-coi} writes a comparison of signed operands on \code|$signed| with a signed constant, a sign extension as \code|$unsigned($signed(x) + 13'sd0)|, a signed shift as \code|$unsigned($signed(a) >>> n)|, and the RTL's own casts. The \code|$unsigned| around an extension matters: alone, \code|$signed(x) + 13'sd0| sits in the relation's unsigned context and is zero-extended, by SystemVerilog and by HARM alike. HARM evaluated that first rendering false on 289 of 502 samples, which the trace oracle, computing with unbounded integers, did not see; this is why HARM's own evaluation became the relations' second oracle. 163 expressions with casts agree with Icarus Verilog, and Z3 is checked on every 4-valued assignment of 9 pairs with casts.

\subsection{Constants outside the reset branch (H26, D-040)}
\ms{H10} made a proposition of a constant only in a reset branch. A constant assigned elsewhere, such as a saturated value (\code|acc <= 12'sd2047|), now gives \code|acc == 12'd2047| as a consequent of the relations' trees (\ms{H25}'s form when it is negative). A constant signal, a 1-bit target (both polarities exist already) and a target with more than 8 distinct constants (a lookup table: \texttt{aes\_cipher} has 5{,}121) are left out. The trees take three propositions since \ms{H26}: the guard that a formal tool required for the negative saturation, \code|en && !clr && $signed(sum) < -13'sd2047|, has three. Mining time was unchanged within noise.

\subsection{One name per net (H34, D-042)}\label{sec:aliases}
A net that crosses a module port is dumped once per scope, so \texttt{harm-coi} gave each copy its own name, and HARM mined the copies' equality (\code!u_i2cSlave.sdaOut |-> u_i2cSlave.u_serialInterface.sdaOut! on AssertLLM2's \texttt{i2c\_slave}) and repeated other assertions once per copy. With \code|--aliases| (\ms{H34}, off by default) two atoms are one net when the RTL makes them so: a port connection, \code|assign a = b| or \code|wire a = b| whose other side is a plain reference to one signal of an equivalent type, the driven side with no other source, neither side unknown or on the clock's path. A connection through logic (\code!.rst(rstSyncToClk | startEdgeDet)!), a select, a constant, a width change or an inout port is not one net, and trace values are never used. Every visible name of a net becomes its shallowest one (fewest scope levels, then string order); predicates, hints, relations and cones use it, and \code|coi.json| lists the merged names under \code|aliases|.

\subsection{Clock domains (H35, D-043)}\label{sec:domains}
\texttt{harm-coi} samples one clock: with several it refuses unless \code|--clock| is given, and then every register on another clock is unknown, with everything it feeds. \code|--domains FILE| (\ms{H35}) maps the domains without choosing a clock. A domain is a clock root, a process's clock followed back through single-source wires and ports, with its edges, registers and resets (the roots of their asynchronous controls); a root driven by logic or a register (a divider, a gate) is derived, from the domains it is made from. A crossing is a register of another domain that reaches a domain's register through wires and logic, and a synchronizer a register fed through wires only by one foreign register, followed by a register fed only by it. With \code|--reset|, each domain lists the given resets its registers read. The other outputs are unchanged.

Without \code|--cut-domains| (\ms{H36}), a register on another clock is unknown, and HARM treats unknown signals as in every cone, so they were mined on the sampling clock's ticks: assertions over two clocks, true only for the trace's clock ratio. With it, every signal of another domain is cut: its registers, its clock, the top inputs only it reads and the signals computed only from those are no target, source, predicate, hint or relation, and the cones stop at them; \code|coi.json| lists them under \code|cut|. A register fed from another domain, such as a synchronizer's first stage, is mined from its own domain's sources. On a single-clock design nothing changes.

\subsection{Elaboration (H23, H28)}
\texttt{harm-coi} gives slang the default timescale \code|--timescale 1ns/1ps|, as trivergence and OpenFPV do, so a design that mixes files with and without a \code|`timescale| directive elaborates (\ms{H28}); a file's own directive still governs it. HARM's keyword list (\ms{H23}) is shared with \texttt{harm-coi}, which writes a signal named after a HARM keyword as an escaped identifier (\code|\W |).
